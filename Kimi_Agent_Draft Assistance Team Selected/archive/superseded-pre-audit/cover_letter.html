Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript entitled “The Causal Geometry of Prediction Errors in Interatomic Potentials: A Hyper-Ribbon Manifold Analysis with Simpson's Paradox Detection” for consideration as a Technical Article in Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation (IMMI). This is a solo-authored work by Alex Welcing (Lupine Science).

What is new. This paper presents the first demonstration that elastic constant prediction errors for 559 interatomic potentials universally occupy hyper-ribbon manifolds with effective dimensionality 1.05–1.86 out of 3 — the signature of sloppy model universality applied to materials simulation. We discover a striking BCC/FCC crystal-structure dichotomy in prediction accuracy, and present the first application of random-effects meta-analysis and Simpson's paradox detection to interatomic potential benchmarking, revealing that standard cross-element aggregation commits ecological fallacy ($I^2 = 98.6\%$). We further show that this hyper-ribbon structure is paradigm-independent by extending our analysis to foundation MLIPs (MACE-MP-0, CHGNet, Orb-v3).

Why IMMI. IMMI is an ideal venue for this work because it uniquely publishes at the intersection of materials informatics, computational methodology, and manufacturing-relevant property prediction — the precise nexus of our contributions. The journal's emphasis on integrative approaches aligns with our goal of bridging statistical methodology with actionable insights for materials simulation.

This manuscript has not been submitted to any other journal for simultaneous consideration. No preprint version has been published. All code and data are available open-source at github.com/alexwelcing/lupine. The standalone analysis engine, atlas-distill, is released under the MIT License.
