Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript, “Hyper-Ribbon Manifolds of Prediction Error: Universal Low-Dimensional Structure in Parameterized Models, with Ecological Fallacy Detection,” for consideration as a Research Article in your journal.

This paper reports a discovery with relevance across physics, mathematics, statistics, and machine learning. By analyzing 559 independently parameterized models for predicting material properties, we demonstrate that prediction errors universally collapse onto low-dimensional hyper-ribbon manifolds — the signature of sloppy model universality. We further show that standard cross-system benchmarking commits ecological fallacy (Simpson's paradox) with extreme heterogeneity ($I^2 = 98.6\%$), and that this hyper-ribbon structure extends to foundation neural network potentials, establishing paradigm independence.

The work synthesizes information geometry, random-effects meta-analysis, and causal inference in a way we believe will interest readers from multiple disciplines. The framing is deliberately general: while the domain is interatomic potentials, the questions (geometric error structure, heterogeneity in multi-model benchmarks, confounder control) are universal.

No preprint has been published. All code and data are open-source (MIT License) at github.com/alexwelcing/lupine. The analysis engine, atlas-distill, is a standalone Rust binary.
