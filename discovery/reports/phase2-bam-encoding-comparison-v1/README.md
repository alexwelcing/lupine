# BAM schema: useful ASCII definitions under two encoding hypotheses

**The schema supports several documentary roles, but no complete unit/uncertainty adapter or scientific admission.** A single independently reviewed comparison strictly decoded the same retained bytes as Windows-1252 and ISO-8859-1. Both parsed under the specified semicolon CSV convention; neither was selected as the actual encoding. The original UTF-8 failure remains unchanged.

The practical findings are that test identity is requested more strongly than specimen identity, calibration provenance is described, and selected endpoint labels/units are available. Critical limits remain: specimen/history identifiers can be optional, current-test proof strength is generic `Rp`, temperature-unit entries are unresolved, and no explicit uncertainty/covariance definition was found in the scoped ASCII technical text.

No new GET or other source request occurred. The [separate scope](scope.json), exact [reader](compare_documentation.py) and independent pre-invocation review were bound before the one [10:01:38 UTC invocation](execution.json).

The [result](result.json) records successful strict decoding and CSV parsing under **each hypothesis**, with 234 CSV records including the header, 347 physical lines and eleven columns per record. Those are document-structure counts, not tests, specimens, independent units or eligible examples. Across these records, including header and empty cells, 2,558 cells were ASCII-invariant and 16 contained non-ASCII text; three of those differed between the hypotheses. The internal full selected-cell projection is retained ignored and must not be published.

Agreement of structure is expected because both single-byte hypotheses preserve ASCII delimiters/quotes and the fixed parser preserves CR/LF framing. It does not independently validate an author-intended encoding or complete format conformance. See [method-limits.md](method-limits.md). Successful parsing is not success of the earlier deferred partial-role profile, which remains unchanged and unimplemented.

A [literal scoped search](ascii-term-check.json) found no occurrence of `uncertainty`, `covariance`, `correlation`, `coverage factor` or `confidence` in the selected ASCII-invariant technical cells. It likewise found no literal batch/heat-number/replicate/repeated terms. This is **scoped lexical absence**, not an exhaustive absence claim: non-ASCII cells, withheld examples/options, external definitions and measurement records could matter. The safe conclusion is that this inspection does not supply those definitions or grouping links.

## Encoding and semantic ambiguities preserved

The two ENTRY cells at r18 and r40 and the example/options cell at r113 differ between hypotheses. No interpretation of their differing characters is promoted. Eight UNIT cells at r24, r46, r52–53, r122–123 and r135–136 contain non-ASCII content and remain unbound even where both codecs produce the same string. Six example/options cells also contain non-ASCII text; the entire examples/options column was withheld from the researcher projection. The literal ASCII question marks in temperature UNIT cells are a separate ambiguity that merely choosing a codec would not repair.

The full raw object, both complete decoded hypothetical texts and all parsed cells were machine-accessible, including potentially empirical examples/options/comments. The researcher inspected selected ASCII fields/comments; some entries contain illustrative numerical conventions and standard references. This is exposed documentation, not an untouched blind source. No arbitrary example values or long translated standard quotations are reproduced here. Withholding the examples column from the projection does not imply its bytes were unread or that comments/entries cannot contain examples.

The record-declared CC BY 4.0 remains distinct from any embedded notice or cited standard's rights. No cited standard, certificate, measurement workbook, LIS, image, XLSX alternative or downstream model was acquired. No source bytes or prior receipt were rewritten.
