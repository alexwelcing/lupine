# Challenge to the proposed next source priority

The proposed Rhizo route is a reasonable priority for a bounded readiness audit because its pinned artifacts already have a completed metadata-custody path. It is not an admitted map-recovery experiment or a fresh screening benchmark. The reviewed catalog evidence does not currently establish a ready fresh paired-screening population; this conclusion is limited to those reviewed routes, not every possible catalog.

AM Bench's five advertised same-alloy specimens across two strain rates are not evidence of five candidate pools. The reviewed readiness note also leaves endpoint derivation and pairing unverified. Pausing that route for the specific screening objective is defensible; small size alone is not a mathematical prohibition on every finite descriptive or engineering use.

The Rhizo custody report records 110 acquired, identity-matched artifacts and all 36 declared material/model IDs, without target-value inspection by that report's researcher. It explicitly leaves numerical completeness, finiteness and scientific eligibility unresolved. These counts support economical follow-up, not empirical readiness.

| Question for the narrower retrospective target | Necessary distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Which archived result is being compared? | Bind the exact stored-observable/reference identity and treatment of alternatives before numerical comparison. The 28 unit-level two-reference vacancy cases remain unresolved; metadata has not established a unique numerical reference. If the target is specifically an existing archived binding, that must be declared rather than silently choosing a new scientifically preferred reference. |
| Which map is being checked? | The fixed figure map, sign, model-geometry source, unit constant and numerical representation must be explicit. The cubic, re-relaxed calculator is a different operation. Fourth-facet linear continuation is an empirical approximation, not an identity established by archived arithmetic. |
| Are labels actually defined? | Named records and schema passes do not establish finite numeric values, complete anchors/output/reference values, convergence or a usable loss for every declared ID. A bounded completeness pass must preserve missingness and competing records, not silently drop difficult cases. |
| Are hard physical bounds required? | They are not a logical prerequisite to comparing fixed archived values under a stated computation. They are still required for the corresponding certified physical-pruning claim. An observed residual, maximum or successful recovery cannot become such a bound. |
| Is an exact checkpoint required? | Unknown checkpoint identity limits reproducing or attributing a result to a particular model checkpoint. It does not automatically invalidate an explicitly artifact-level comparison of already stored numbers; the claim must remain at that narrower level. |
| What are the units and evidence costs? | Thirty-six material/model IDs are not 36 independent experiments. Four models may reuse one reference; 43 matched reference locations are archive locations, not independently established physical measurements. Preserve shared-reference provenance and unique-evidence accounting. |
| What scientific result could follow? | One historical fourth-facet output per separate field block supports a possible retrospective map-recovery question. It does not establish competing held-out candidates under a shared field, cross-material transfer or fresh predictive superiority. |

Reference rights, declared versus verified identities, reconstruction/condition caveats and previous exposure still need honest treatment. A finite archive-value estimand need not solve every question required for physical truth, but it must state what remains unresolved rather than imply those questions disappeared. Metadata-based provenance reconciliation is different from accessing the mixed numeric bodies; that next access boundary must remain explicit.

The next useful question is whether one bounded provenance/reference/completeness pass can produce a defensible fixed retrospective target. Its outcome may be a narrower comparison, unresolved status or a stop. This addendum supplies no extraction permission, reference choice, threshold, allocation, sampling claim or new protocol. The finite counting theorem applies only if its separate sampling premises are actually implemented; an existing archive or a new audit is not evidence that a uniform draw occurred. Existing MPEA/JARVIS negatives and the prohibition on retuning exposed outcomes are unchanged.
