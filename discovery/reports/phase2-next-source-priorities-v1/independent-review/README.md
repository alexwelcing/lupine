# Finite archive labels and the role of grouping

The narrower mathematical claim is sound: a fixed finite archive-label audit can use uniform sampling without replacement without assuming physically independent rows. This does not establish that Li is ready for that audit, remove current source-access gates, or support future-material prediction.

For a frozen residual frame of `M` distinct IDs, a fixed policy and one well-defined binary loss per ID, let `K` be the fixed number of losses. A uniform size-`e` subset has failure count probability

`Pr(X=x) = choose(K,x) choose(M-K,e-x) / choose(M,e)`.

This counts subsets of labeled IDs. Physical correlation, overlapping publications or duplicate underlying measurements do not enter the count. Probability is over the evaluation draw, conditional on the fixed frame and policy; it is not a claim about how materials or measurements were generated. Duplicates change the ID-weighted target and do not create independent measurement evidence.

| Question | What the narrower claim needs | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sampling units | Exact unique archive IDs and the weighting implied by sampling those IDs. If units are panels, their candidate memberships and overlap must be frozen. | Distinct physical specimens, materials, casts, publications or independent studies. |
| Labels | A fixed recorded-data endpoint, evaluator, ranking/tie rules where relevant, and a well-defined loss for every framed ID. Selected losses must be ascertainable under the declared rule. | Latent physical truth, correct measurement uncertainty or suitability for a plant. |
| Allocation | An actual uniform size-`e` draw from the declared remainder, with fixed count and no outcome-dependent dropping, refill, stopping, reranking or repeated-test selection. | A random-looking hash order, exchangeability of a future scientific population or a bound conditional on the fully realized evaluation subset. |
| Calibration versus evaluation | Evaluation of an already frozen policy has the finite counting law. The separate marginal calibration result also requires a role-independent fixed panel-score map and its stated random allocation law. | Calibration-specific fitting cannot be repaired merely by shuffling IDs. Shared calibration does not make evaluation outcomes IID Bernoulli. |
| Grouping and leakage | Grouping is structurally necessary when it defines the claimed panels, exclusions or development/evaluation split. Known shared evidence must be accounted for in provenance and acquisition costs. | The counting theorem alone does not make a source-held-out test fresh, resolve training overlap or justify counting duplicates as separately purchased evidence. |

“Fixed labels” does not require inspecting every reserved numeric outcome before drawing a sample. It requires a stable, well-defined labeling rule and underlying recorded target. Unknown evaluation truth is not success. Whole-frame completeness or a separately frozen role-blind restriction may establish a particular finite target; dropping incomplete selected panels does not implement the same sampling design.

For Li, proving physical independence of source groups is therefore **not logically necessary solely for an explicitly ID-weighted fixed-archive estimand**. However, if the proposed endpoint is all-minimizer retention within source panels, unresolved grouping can leave the panels and their losses undefined. If the claim concerns distinct materials/studies or fresh source holdout, grouping and overlap evidence remain necessary to support that claim. A stable arbitrary ID partition would not prove any of those scientific meanings. Endpoint semantics, units, pairing claims, missingness and source rights likewise remain substantive audit questions; the counting theorem cannot settle them.

A fixed finite-policy audit and a fresh predictive study answer different questions. A policy's fixed archive performance is not automatically invalidated mathematically by historical label exposure, but that observation neither authorizes outcome-informed development nor repairs a held-out claim. The current prohibition on tuning exposed MPEA/JARVIS populations and all existing negative results remain unchanged. The historical next-independent-study recommendation targets a distinct scientific-transfer objective; its independent-study requirements should not be misrepresented as prerequisites of the narrower finite counting identity.

The permissible prioritization consequence is to separate two gates: whether a precise recorded-archive target and valid sampling design can be frozen, and whether source lineage and sampling justify an independent-study or future-material claim. This note chooses neither a source nor a protocol and supplies no new readiness, counts, thresholds, acquisition permission or execution result.
