## What a finite label claim would and would not require

The [finite-archive design](../../reports/phase2-finite-archive-design-v1/README.md),
section 5, already supplies the relevant distinction. After fixing a policy and
residual frame of `M` IDs with fixed binary failure labels, a uniform size-`e`
sample without replacement has a hypergeometric failure count. **Physical
independence of those IDs is not required.** A census observes the full finite
label fraction without sampling inference. No measurement-uncertainty interval
is required merely to compare recorded labels as recorded labels. This does not
make them error-free measurements of latent truth.

The frame, endpoint pairing/meaning, missingness handling, loss and sampling law
must nevertheless be well defined. For calibration, the score construction must
also remain invariant to reserved role assignment. Unknown Li source grouping is
not by itself a mathematical veto on an ID-weighted label audit; grouping is
still needed to define panels when panels are the unit, or to support distinct
study/material, leakage, fresh-holdout and unique-evidence cost claims. Repeated
archive rows may receive repeated finite-frame weight if stated explicitly;
they must not be sold as additional independent experiments. Lookup counts and
unique physical evidence costs are different quantities.

Historical exposure does not make finite arithmetic impossible. It prevents an
honest claim of prospective superiority when the policy or target was selected
using those outcomes. No exposed MPEA/JARVIS policy, allocation or threshold is
changed here; their negative results remain unchanged. Future-material risk,
plant suitability and conditional risk after selection require additional
probability and physical premises absent from a finite archive audit.
