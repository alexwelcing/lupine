# Source-free hypothesis challenges prepared before candidate review

These are mathematical review questions, not empirical results, selected scientific criteria or a proposed benchmark. No source data, model or study execution is involved.

Let C denote a valid joint measurement-coverage event, A denote acceptance and T denote true feasibility. Even if the rule satisfies A ∩ C ⊆ T, the resulting statement is only P(A ∩ not T) ≤ P(not C). It does not by itself bound P(not T | A). For any abstract 0 < α < 1, consider T always false, P(A)=α and C=not A. Then P(C)=1−α and the inclusion holds, yet P(not T | A)=1. The accepted-pool denominator and its conditions matter. This is a finite probabilistic counterexample, not a chosen material threshold or a claim about BAM errors.

Likewise, marginal coverage is not simultaneous coverage. If each of m failure events has probability α, their union may have probability α when the events coincide, mα when they are disjoint and mα≤1, or 1−(1−α)^m under an additionally justified independence premise. Identical marginal summaries do not distinguish those couplings. Any proposed bound must name its event and conditioning and state which premise supplies the marginal probabilities.

A publisher's expanded measurement uncertainty and k=2 statement is documentary evidence of the publisher's metrology assertion. It does not alone establish a Gaussian error law, that U/2 is an independently verified standard deviation, a per-row conditional coverage guarantee, a cross-endpoint covariance matrix, or a calibrated predictive error distribution. The contract should leave these as explicit unestablished premises unless separately supported.

A fixed list of test identifiers establishes documentary pairing within that source. Random sampling, exchangeability, repeated calibration units, independent casts/heats and future deployment distribution remain separate assumptions. A theorem about a named probability space can be correct while its BAM applicability remains blocked.
