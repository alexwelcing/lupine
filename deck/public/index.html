02 — The proof

One law, tested at three layers of the matter stack.

The research program found a geometric law: models in a family share one error direction, and that direction fingerprints whatever constraint binds the family — the functional form, the training data, the reference theory. We tested it the hard way: pre-registered experiments with kill conditions committed to version control before any model ran. Neither kill condition was triggered.

  1. L1

    Classical potentials — 40 years, one geometry.

    559 interatomic potentials, 15 metals. Within a model family, prediction errors correlate at 0.95 — and the error geometry has not changed in forty years of development. Accuracy improved; the constraint signature did not. You cannot fit your way off a constraint surface.

    Verified
  2. L2

    AI foundation models inherit their training physics.

    Four neural architectures × two quantum-chemistry training theories, pre-registered. Errors cluster by training theory, not by architecture (permutation p = 0.029) — and retraining on the better theory rotates the error exactly as predicted. The model zoo is one family in disguise.

    Verified
  3. L3

    Even the simulators obey it.

    12 DFT implementations, 384 crystals (public verification data). Errors cluster by the shared approximation, not by which code computed them (p = 0.017): four independent codes sharing one pseudopotential align at 0.70–0.87, while the same code under a different approximation drops to 0.21.

    Verified
  4. Σ

    Why this is a business, not just a result.

    Because the shared error is one direction, one correction vector repairs every model in a family at once — a rank-one calibration removes ~96% of foundation-model elastic error without retraining, and it transfers to models that don't exist yet. Cross-model agreement becomes a zero-cost trust gauge: it tells you when an ensemble can be calibrated and when it is lying to you. The theory behind both claims is machine-checked in Lean 4 — a 180-theorem corpus, zero unproven obligations — and the entire evidence chain replays from public data in two commands.

    Verified

What this number means

$7.0T is the probability-weighted EV across the seven scenarios above, conditional on execution (probabilities sum to 1.0). Multiply by 50% — the unconditional probability the company doesn't fail outright, priced explicitly in the floor — and you get a ~$3.5T unconditional ceiling. That is roughly ~10,000× the floor's $332M base DCF: four orders of magnitude. The point is not the number — it is that the floor measures the wrong altitude. Most probability mass sits in the lower, near-term scenarios; the giant EVs carry small, explicitly-directional probabilities.
