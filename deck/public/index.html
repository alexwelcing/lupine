01 / What we believe

The ideals that pull the work.

Before the arc, before the stack, before any math: the commitments that tell you what kind of company this is. None of them are negotiable. All of them are testable against what we actually ship.

01

Scarcity is an engineering problem.

The 20th century treated material scarcity as a fixed feature of reality and built economic theory around it. The 21st century has the science to dissolve it — atomistic prediction, generative matter, autonomous synthesis, atomic-precision manufacturing. The Replicator is a feasible engineering target on a 30-year horizon, not a metaphor for one.

Directional
02

The audit layer is a public good.

Foundation models for matter need to be trustworthy before they can be deployed at civilization scale. The methodology that names where they fail compounds for everyone or it compounds for no one. We ship Apache 2.0 because anything else makes the substrate brittle. Closed validation does not survive contact with reality.

Verified
03

Truth before product.

The first artifact was a peer-reviewed paper — Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation 2026, in press. The methodology will be cited longer than any single deployment of it. We measure the physics first, then we build the engine. Reverse the order and you ship hallucination dressed as software.

Verified
04

The right timescale is 30 years.

Civilization-scale problems do not fit a venture fund's LP horizon. The math an investor can score in five years is the floor — what happens if Lupine becomes a respectable software-of-record company. The platform a generation can build over thirty years is the ceiling. We optimize for the platform; the math just has to clear.

Directional
05

Sovereignty and openness are the same commitment.

You cannot expropriate methodology you can read. Open-core is the only architecture that lets sovereign-mandated buyers — CHIPS-funded foundries, ITAR-eligible defense programs, MGI national labs — trust the substrate they depend on. The closed alternative gets banned in their procurement before it gets bought.

Triangulated
06

Compounding learning beats compounding capital.

Every customer engagement leaves training signal. The benchmark improves. The methodology paper improves. The next customer gets a better engine than the previous one did. The moat is not the contract; the moat is that we are the canonical place where the field agrees on what counts as truth.

Directional
07

Step 1 is unfashionable.

Generative matter is on the cover of the magazine. Autonomous synthesis is the flagship NSF program. Atomic-precision manufacturing has the dramatic name. The audit layer underneath is invisible, slow, load-bearing. We took it because it is load-bearing. Step 1 is the part everyone wants to skip; that is exactly why it is the seat to claim.

Directional

Why phase 1 is the load-bearing one

Generative models for matter are roughly where AlphaFold-1 was in 2018: clearly working, not yet trustworthy. Without a layer that names where atomistic predictions fail and feeds those failures back into the next generation, phase 2 stacks generative hallucination on top of unmeasured prediction error and the whole pipeline gets worse, not better. Lupine ships the methodology that makes phase 1 trustworthy — and that methodology compounds into training signal for every layer above it.

03 / Where this sits

We are the validation substrate for the matter stack.

The applications are visible. The generative models are fashionable. The compute primitives are 30 years old and improving. The audit layer in the middle — the one that catches when a foundation model proposes a structure that physics rejects, and compresses the error into corrections — is structurally underweight in today's ecosystem. That is the layer Lupine ships.

What this number means

$7.0T is the probability-weighted EV across the seven scenarios above, conditional on execution (probabilities sum to 1.0). Multiply by 50% — the unconditional probability the company doesn't fail outright, priced explicitly in the floor — and you get a ~$3.5T unconditional ceiling. That is roughly ~10,000× the floor's $332M base DCF: four orders of magnitude. The point is not the number — it is that the floor measures the wrong altitude. Most of the probability mass sits in the lower, near-term scenarios; the giant EVs carry small, explicitly-directional probabilities.
